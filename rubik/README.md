# God's number of the 3x3x3 Rubik's cube (half-turn metric) in Lean 4

Lean 4.33.1 + Mathlib. Everything listed under "Proved" is sorry-free, uses no
`native_decide`, and `#print axioms` reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice`,
`Quot.sound` (the `#print axioms` lines are at the end of both files). All finite
computations are `decide` / `decide +kernel`, i.e. checked by the kernel.

**Result: `18 ≤ godsNumber ≤ 53`.** The true value 20 (Rokicki, Kociemba, Davidson,
Dethridge 2010) is *not* proved; see the feasibility estimates at the end.

## Stage 1: the model (`Cube.lean`)

- `Cube`: a structure `cp : Perm (Fin 8)`, `co : Fin 8 → ZMod 3`, `ep : Perm (Fin 12)`,
  `eo : Fin 12 → ZMod 2` (all `8!·3^8·12!·2^12` assemblies), with a `Group` instance:
  `(a * b).cp = a.cp * b.cp`, `(a * b).co i = a.co (b.cp i) + b.co i`, same for edges.
  Kociemba's numbering: corners `URF UFL ULB UBR DFR DLF DBL DRB`, edges
  `UR UF UL UB DR DF DL DB FR FL BL BR`.
- `q : Face → Cube`: the six clockwise quarter turns, given by explicit tables.
- `Move` = face × `Fin 3` (quarter, half, three-quarter turn): 18 moves (`card_move`).
- `evalSeq : List Move → Cube` (product), `Reachable s := ∃ l, evalSeq l = s`,
  `dist s := sInf {n | ∃ l, l.length = n ∧ evalSeq l = s}`,
  `godsNumber := sSup (dist '' {s | Reachable s})`.
- `godsNumber_spec : (∀ s, Reachable s → dist s ≤ godsNumber) ∧ ∃ s, Reachable s ∧ dist s = godsNumber`,
  so `godsNumber` is the maximum; `le_godsNumber_iff`, `godsNumber_le_iff` give the
  `∃` / `∀` formulations.
- Sanity checks: `q_pow_four` (`q f ^ 4 = 1`), `q_pow_two_ne`, `sexy_six`
  (`(R U R' U')^6 = 1`), `sexy_three_ne`, `superflip_eq` (the 20-move sequence
  `U R2 F B R B2 R U2 L B2 R U' D' R2 F R' L B2 U2 F2` gives the superflip),
  `dist_superflip_le : dist superflip ≤ 20`, `superflip_central`.

## Stage 2: invariants

`Valid s := ∑ i, s.co i = 0 ∧ ∑ i, s.eo i = 0 ∧ sign s.cp = sign s.ep`.

- `Valid.mul`, `valid_q`, `Valid.mul_q` (a face turn preserves the three invariants),
  `Reachable.valid : Reachable s → Valid s`.
- `not_reachable_twist`, `not_reachable_flip`, `not_reachable_swap`.
- `card_valid : Nat.card {s // Valid s} = 43252003274489856000` (kernel of the homomorphism
  `chi` to `ZMod 3 × ZMod 2 × ℤˣ`, which has index 12).

## Stage 3: the group order and the lower bound

- `exists_reachable_finset`: a kernel-checked stabilizer chain (one cubie per level, 20 levels,
  258 words found by a Minkwitz-style search) gives a `Finset` of `43252003274489856000`
  reachable states, each with `dist ≤ 164`. The checker tracks single cubies through the
  words, so no state is ever enumerated.
- **`reachable_iff_valid : Reachable s ↔ Valid s`** and
  **`card_reachable : Nat.card {s // Reachable s} = 43252003274489856000`**.
- Counting: `C k o` is the set of states produced by canonical sequences of length `k` ending
  in face `o` (no two consecutive moves on one face; on one axis, `U/R/F` before `D/L/B`).
  `C_step` / `evalSeq_mem_C` prove that every sequence can be made canonical without getting
  longer (turns of one face merge, opposite faces commute);
  `card_ball_17 : (ball 17).card ≤ 19973266111335481264`, where `ball 17` contains every reachable
  state with `dist ≤ 17` (`mem_ball_of_dist_le`).
- **`exists_dist_ge_18 : ∃ s, Reachable s ∧ 18 ≤ dist s`** and
  **`eighteen_le_godsNumber : 18 ≤ godsNumber`**
  (`19973266111335481264 < 43252003274489856000`; 18 is the best this counting argument gives,
  since there are more than `2.6·10^20` canonical sequences of length ≤ 18).
- `godsNumber_bounds : 18 ≤ godsNumber ∧ godsNumber ≤ 164` (the upper bound is the sum of the
  longest word per level of the stabilizer chain).

## Stage 4: a Thistlethwaite-style upper bound (`Thistle.lean`)

**`godsNumber_mem_Icc : 18 ≤ godsNumber ∧ godsNumber ≤ 53`**, also
`godsNumber_le_53`, `dist_le_53 : Reachable s → dist s ≤ 53`.

The chain of subgroups and the kernel-checked tables of coset representatives
(breadth-first search in Python; `d` = longest word):

| level | from → to | cosets | d |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | all states → `HA` = edges unflipped | 2048 | 7 |
| L2s | `HA` → `HS` = ... and E-slice edges in the E slice | 495 | 5 |
| L2c | `HS` → `HC` = ... and corners untwisted (`= ⟨U,D,R2,L2,F2,B2⟩`) | 2187 | 10 |
| L3 | `HC` → `HD3` = ... and corner tetrads, M slice and the twelve corner 4-sets preserved (the cubie pattern of the squares group) | 29400 | 13 |
| L4 | `HD3` → `HE` = ... and all corners solved | 96 | 4 |
| L5 | `HE` → 1 | 6912 | 14 |

Sum of the `d` column: 53; product of the coset counts: `43252003274489856000`.
`level_step` / `chain_step2` (generic): if the words of a table lie in `H`, have length `≤ d`,
and have pairwise distinct values of a key `K` with `K (x * y) = K x` for `y ∈ H'`, then
`|table · P| = |table| · |P|` for any finite `P ⊆ H'`. Since the product of the sizes equals the
group order (`card_valid`), every reachable state is a product of one representative per
level. Keys are sums over cubies of weights (`keyG`), plus for L3 a sum over twelve 4-sets of
corners (`qkey`). The kernel evaluates a word by tracking the 20 cubies through it with
table lookups on `Nat` codes (`trackN`, `trackP`, `stepN`; `stepN_spec` ties the table to the
model); distinctness of keys is checked as "strictly increasing".

Compared with Thistlethwaite's 7 + 10 + 13 + 15 = 45, Thistlethwaite's second and fourth phases are each split in two
(5 + 10 and 4 + 14) to keep the tables small (41 138 words instead of 1.8 million).

## Kernel timings (this machine, 4 shared cores)

- `Cube.lean`: 17 s wall in total (Mathlib import ≈ 4 s). All of stages 1-3.
- Abstract model (`Equiv.Perm`, `ZMod`): ≈ 150 µs per cubie per move (60 000 steps in 9.5 s).
- `Nat`-coded lookup (`stepN`, raw recursors): ≈ 50-100 µs per cubie per move; a raw `Nat.rec`
  loop with about six `Nat` operations costs ≈ 50 µs per iteration; a `match` on a `Nat`
  literal with 18 arms costs ≈ 360 µs. So the kernel does about `10^4` cubie-moves per second,
  i.e. about 500 full-state moves per second.
- `Thistle.lean`: ≈ 525 s wall with the standard command (it sets `Elab.async false`, so it runs
  on one core), 3.1 GB peak anonymous memory; 41 138 words, 365 329 table moves in total, i.e.
  ≈ 1.4 ms per table move (20 cubies, membership test and key). With parallel elaboration
  (default `Elab.async`) the same file took 552 s but 8.8 GB.
- Memory lesson: the elaborator keeps roughly 10-17 kB per numeral of source, so tables are
  stored as one base-32 numeral per word; left-nested `++` of chunks is quadratic in the kernel.

## What is not proved, and what it would cost

1. **`godsNumber ≤ 45` (plain Thistlethwaite)** with the same certificate format needs the
   unsplit tables: 1 082 565 words (≈ 9.5 M moves) and 663 552 words (≈ 8 M moves). At the
   measured rate (≈ 1.4 ms per table move) that is ≈ 7 hours of kernel time and a ≈ 30 MB source;
   feasible as an overnight job, not within one hour. Certificates that share prefixes (a BFS
   tree: 20 lookups per word instead of 20 per move) would cut this about tenfold, but then the
   keys no longer come out sorted and a verified sort of `10^6` keys is needed.
2. **`godsNumber ≤ 29/30` (Kociemba two-phase, Reid 1995)**: the second phase group has
   `1.95·10^10` elements; no explicit table. It needs a verified search with pruning tables,
   and the kernel has no arrays (a lookup in an `n`-bit `Nat` costs `O(n)`). Estimated
   `10^10`-`10^11` kernel steps at best: years of CPU. Not attempted.
3. **`20 ≤ godsNumber` (Reid's superflip proof)**: one must show that no sequence of ≤ 19 moves
   gives the superflip. Meet in the middle needs the ≈ `2.5·10^11` states within 10 moves
   (≈ `2.6·10^9` after dividing by the 96 symmetries/inversion that fix the superflip),
   each costing ≥ 2 ms to generate, plus a verified sort/merge: ≥ 60 CPU-days of kernel time
   and a formalised symmetry reduction. Reid's own search (Kociemba phase-1 enumeration) is
   of the same order. Not feasible in a session; plausible only as a dedicated multi-week
   project with a much faster certificate checker.
4. **`godsNumber = 20`**: the 2010 proof solved 55 882 296 symmetry-reduced cosets of
   `⟨U,D,R2,L2,F2,B2⟩`, each with 19.5 billion positions, in ≈ 35 CPU-years of optimised C.
   The kernel is `10^4`-`10^5` times slower than such code, so a kernel-checked replay is out
   of reach (≥ `10^5` CPU-years); it would need a verified external checker, which the rules
   here (no `native_decide`) exclude.

    cd tools
